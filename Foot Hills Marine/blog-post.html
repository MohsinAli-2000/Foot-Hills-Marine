Navigation basics

A beginner’s guide to reading buoys, markers, and channel signs

Learn the most common aids to navigation, how to identify safe passage, and when to slow down before you enter a new waterway.

September 8, 2026 6 min read

Share

f in

Aids to navigation are the road signs of the water. Once you understand their colors, shapes, and numbers, they turn an unfamiliar channel into a clear, readable route.

Start with red and green

Lateral markers show the edges of a navigable channel. When returning from open water or traveling upstream, keep red markers on your right and green markers on your left. Boaters remember it as “red, right, returning.”

The rule to remember

“Red, right, returning.” Keep red markers to starboard as you come in from open water.

Read the shape and number

Red markers are usually triangular and carry even numbers. Green markers are square and carry odd numbers. The numbers increase as you move inland, helping you confirm both your direction and position.

  • Red nun buoys

    Triangular top, even number. Keep to starboard when returning.

  • Green can buoys

    Square or flat top, odd number. Keep to port when returning.

  • Red and green markers

    The top color marks the preferred channel at a junction.

Slow down and look ahead

Markers can shift with weather and water levels, so never rely on one sign alone. Match what you see with your chart, watch your depth, and reduce speed whenever the channel narrows or traffic builds.

Local conditions always come first. If a marker looks damaged, missing, or out of position, give it extra room and report it to the local authority.
